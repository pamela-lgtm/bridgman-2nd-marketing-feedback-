Section Two

What prospects actually told us

Becky follows up personally after every showing. Here is what came back, in their words rather than ours.

Prospect one — still in contact

Asked whether you would add a washer and dryer to the garage apartment. You agreed, and we thank you for that — it removes a genuine objection and we have added it to the listing.

They had planned the apartment for a family member, and that person is now unsure of their plans. They said they would be in touch. We are following up, but their own circumstances are the variable here rather than anything about the home.

Prospect two — chose another home

Toured, then leased elsewhere. When asked why, they said only that the other property was a better fit. No criticism of your home, and nothing specific we can act on.

Prospect three — compared two of our homes and chose the other

This one is the most useful. She toured your home and one of our Eagle Creek properties and told us she liked both. She is leaning toward the other home because it sits on the golf course and has a pool.

Read those together. Not one prospect raised the condition, the presentation or the home itself as a concern. Two of the three toured, liked it, and chose or are choosing somewhere else.

That is a value comparison, and it is the single most important thing in this report. A renter standing between two homes is asking which one is the better use of their money. When the other home has a pool and a golf course position, your home has to win that comparison somewhere else — and the only place left is the number.

We cannot add a pool. We cannot move the home onto a golf course. What we can do is make sure that when a renter weighs the two, yours is clearly the better value.

Section Four

There are only three levers, and two of them are fixed

Whether a rental leases comes down to three things, in this order.

Location — fixed, and excellent

Laureate Park is one of the strongest rental submarkets in Central Florida. Nothing to improve and nothing working against you.

Condition — fixed, and excellent

Your home is beautifully presented, and the showing feedback confirms it. One prospect described it as gorgeous. This is not a condition problem.

Price — the only lever we can actually move

Two of the three variables are already working in your favor and cannot be improved. That leaves one, and it is the one that resolves everything else. When a home is priced correctly, location and condition do their work. When it is above the market, nothing else compensates.

This is why we are not suggesting new photographs, a different description, or more marketing spend. Those would be treating a symptom that does not exist.

Section Five

The season is working against us now

Timing matters more at this point in the year than at any other, and it is the reason we would rather act this week than next month.

Rental demand in this corridor runs on the school calendar. Families relocating for the school year finished moving in August. From October into the holidays, fewer households move — and the ones who do have more homes to choose from.

A home at this size and price point already draws from a smaller pool than a typical three-bedroom. Entering the slowest stretch of the year narrows that pool further.

A price that looks competitive today looks ordinary in six weeks and expensive by December. If we are going to move, moving now is worth considerably more than moving later.

There is one more reason not to wait. A listing receives more attention in its first two weeks than at any other point in its life, because every renter actively searching sees it as new inventory. We are at the end of that window now. After this, a home that renters have already seen repeatedly stops reading as available and starts reading as passed over — and that is a much harder position to price out of.

Section Seven

Our recommendation

Reduce to $5,150, effective immediately

This is a decision we are asking you to make this week, and we would like to implement it before the weekend so the home is repriced for the strongest viewing days.

The reduction is modest by design. The activity tells us we are close — renters are touring and responding well. We are not chasing the market down; we are removing the last objection standing between a strong showing and a signed application.

What happens next

We measure the following five days closely — inquiries, showings booked and showings attended. If the response is not strong enough for us to choose a well-qualified resident rather than accept whoever applies, we will come back to you with a further recommendation rather than letting it drift.

We would simply want you to make that decision knowing that each additional week costs approximately $1,208 in rent that is never recoverable — and that a week in October is worth more than a week in December.
